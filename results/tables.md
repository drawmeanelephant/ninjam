## 1. Fixed propagation delay (emission to playback)

`delay = median over markers of (heard_session_pos - k*mark_period)`.
This is the constant that the interval model contributes; drift is measured
relative to it. A value near a multiple of the interval is the interval
structure showing through.

## 2. Pairwise interval-alignment error and clock drift

`align(k) = max over client pairs of err - min over client pairs of err`,
for the same marker k: how far apart the clients are, in ms.
`align mod` repeats that after wrapping every pair's error into
+/- half an interval, i.e. it ignores whole-interval slips -- that is the
error of the grid itself, which is what the interval model controls.
`drift` is a least-squares fit of err against wall time, in ms/min, for the
pair with the largest |drift|. Predicted drift for a pair is
`0.06 * (ppm_listener - ppm_emitter)` ms/min.

## 5. Clock domain: interval-boundary phase, no audio involved

for every client and differenced between pairs. No audio involved: this
is the mechanism underneath the numbers above.

The difference is reported twice. `raw` is the plain millisecond
difference, so a pair that is one whole interval apart reads as ~4000.
`wrapped` folds that into +/- half an interval, which is the real phase
error. `slip rate` is the fraction of samples whose RAW difference is
more than a quarter of an interval -- i.e. the pair had slipped a whole
interval rather than being slightly out of phase.

`markers skipped at emit` should be 0: a non-zero value means a marker was
scheduled into a gap in the client's own sample stream and never sent.

`shadow rows` are duplicate (listener, emitter, k) triples. A Hann template
aligned half a burst late still correlates about 0.5 with the burst, so
every marker throws a weak shadow roughly one burst length behind itself.
They are excluded above -- the strongest hit wins -- and they are not
evidence of anything NINJAM did. The detector now suppresses them at
source, so re-running with the current code gives 0 here.
